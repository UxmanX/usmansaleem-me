The gate at the top is a bi-parting sliding gate: the same motion as the automatic sliding entrances I coordinate every day. Slide it open and a fluid solver written for this page takes over. Three hypodermic needles release ink the way Osborne Reynolds did in 1883, so you can watch the streamlines squeeze through the opening, the vena contracta that sets the discharge coefficient.

General notes

  1. Gate. Two leaves on a belt drive part from the centreline, like the automatic sliding entrances (Besam SL500 family) on the 50+ concurrent replacement projects I coordinate at ASSA ABLOY Entrance Systems. This one holds back water instead of weather. Auto cycle runs it like a door radar: open, hold, close.
  2. Dye. Three hypodermic needles release ink filaments, as Osborne Reynolds did in 1883. In still water they hang from the tips; open the gate and they bend towards the opening and squeeze through the vena contracta. Below it the filament breaks up into the water and fades in the sump. Press and hold anywhere in the water to add your own.
  3. Baffle. 6061-T6 aluminium box section, 120 × 40 × 3 mm, cantilevered from a column. Root stress and tip deflection come from Euler–Bernoulli beam theory, loaded by the pressure the simulated water puts on it: the hand check I run before trusting a finite element model (MEng, Finite Element Analysis).
  4. Coating. Switch the baffle between hydrophilic and superhydrophobic. Contact angle decides whether water clings and sheets off the lip or beads and bounces (MEng, Surfaces & Coatings).
  5. Weir and pump. Tagged like a process flow diagram, the documents I checked line by line while commissioning a flour-milling and pasta plant with Bühler, coordinating about 60 technicians on site.
  6. Discharge. While the head stands well above the opening, the measured coefficient settles near 0.6, the textbook value for a sharp-edged orifice. Once the tank drains below the gate width, the opening stops acting like an orifice: the coefficient falls and Torricelli overpredicts the flow. That gap is physics, not a bug.
  7. Solver. 2-D FLIP/PIC fluid (Zhu & Bridson) on a staggered grid, written in plain JavaScript for this page and rendered with WebGL2. Same habit as my audit tool that turned a three-hour portal crawl into fifteen seconds: automate the tedious part, then check it against first principles.
  8. Illustrative. A 2-D model at lab-flume scale (120 mm deep), not a design calculation or a product rating.
